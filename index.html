Architecture
Three-Stage Detection Pipeline

Two fundamentally different detectors run in parallel, then their results are merged into union and intersection views so you can trade precision for recall as needed.

Stage 1

PCA Preprocessing

Raw UNSW-NB15 features are standardized and compressed into principal components that explain 95% of variance. This strips noise and prevents high-magnitude columns like sbytes from dominating the space.

Stage 2A

HDBSCAN Clustering

Density-based clustering groups normal traffic into stable clusters. Anything that doesn't belong to any cluster — label -1 — is a candidate anomaly. No cluster count needed upfront.

Stage 2B

Isolation Forest

Randomly builds isolation trees and scores each point by how few splits it takes to isolate. Anomalies isolate quickly. Provides an orthogonal signal to density-based detection.

Key Findings
What We Learned

Takeaways from running the ensemble on a 1,000-row stratified sample of UNSW-NB15.

Ensemble outperforms either detector alone. Union achieved F1 = 0.344 vs. 0.310 for HDBSCAN solo — two complementary algorithms catch things neither would catch alone.
Intersection is the high-confidence path. At 74.3% precision, points flagged by both detectors independently are very likely real attacks, making them safe to escalate without much analyst triage.
PCA compressed 117 features while preserving signal. HDBSCAN found 11 stable behavioral clusters in the reduced space, showing that network traffic has meaningful structure even without labels.
Recall is the open problem. At 19–23%, a majority of labeled attacks were missed. A larger training window, finer HDBSCAN tuning, or additional features (e.g., inter-arrival jitter) would likely push recall up significantly.
